A day with a 1:1

The rhythm Jamie set: every 1:1 ends with at most 3 actions, each with a date, checked at the next one.

  1. 10amAva sends a prep for each trainer you're seeing that day. A 1:1 before about 10:45 comes at 10am the weekday before.
  2. In the 1:1Lead with the numbers, not "how's it going". Start with the biggest gold mine, then the questions the prep gives you. If they say they're doing fine, the data in the prep is your answer.
  3. Agree 3 actionsThe report suggests three. Change them if you like; each one needs a date (usually the next 1:1).
  4. Book the next 1:1Weekly under 15 hours, fortnightly at 15-20, monthly at 20+. The report tells you which.
  5. Tell Ava how it wentOne message, e.g. "alex went well, he'll finish sam and lily's kickstarters by thursday". Ava saves it, sets the actions and ticks off the old ones.

What the deep prep checks

So you don't have to take "I'm doing fine" on trust. Every finding shows its numbers and where they came from, and anything that's only a likely reason is labelled "Possible reason".

  • Money and hours, now and since they started: profit and ongoing hours over the last 4 normal weeks (holidays and part weeks left out), against 12 weeks and their whole time here, plus how many clients they train each month.
  • Every step of the sale: free session, Kickstarter, strategy session, ongoing, and who's still training at 3 and 6 months, each against the team. The biggest gap in dollars is the gold mine.
  • Their clients and the app: who opens JF Coach, logs workouts and food, shows progress, and gets a reply to their messages. Kickstarter clients who track something themselves go ongoing about twice as often.
  • Program quality: programs past 6 weeks, workouts that open with curls or raises before the big lift, missing rep targets, copied blocks, and whether lifts are actually going up.
  • The free session notes: whether they capture the goal, make the offer, handle the objection and book the next step, and how that lines up with who buys.
  • Showing up: 1:1s and team meetings, cancelled or moved sessions, availability against bookings, admin on time.

Make it yours

You know what helps in a 1:1 better than anyone. Tell Ava what's useful, what isn't, and what you wish it showed. This keeps changing to suit you.

"Send it the night before." "Just text, no picture." "An hour before each 1:1." Ava changes it on the spot.

Private by design

  • Only you and Jamie can open the Trainers tab. Trainers and VAs can't open it and don't see it in their menu.
  • Ava only talks about it with you and Jamie, and only in your private chat, never in a group.
  • Nothing here messages a trainer. What you share with a trainer in a 1:1 is your call.
  • When Jamie has a gripe about a trainer, he tells Ava or Mia and it lands on that trainer's checklist, so nothing gets lost and you don't need a meeting to hear it.

Where the numbers come from

  • Money: the P&L margin per trainer (client payments less GST, wages, super and rent), paid weeks only. Payroll lands a week or two after the money comes in, so the newest weeks fill in later.
  • Hours: ongoing client sessions in JF Coach (a 2-on-1 counts for each client), averaged over 4 weeks.
  • Sales: free session to Kickstarter = Kickstarters sold (HubSpot) ÷ clients who had a free session (JF Coach), last 90 days. Kickstarter to ongoing only counts clients with a decision; ones still waiting show as "no outcome recorded" so they get followed up.
  • Clients and admin: the retention scores, programs, measurements and messages in JF Coach and the portal; free-session notes from the portal, the app form and JF Notes (a note counts if it's there within a day, before the follow-up).
  • 1:1s: your Google Calendar ("1:1 Check In (Name)").
  • Rebuilt every hour from 6am to 9pm. If something looks wrong, tell Ava and it gets checked.
